# Ukraine Civic Tech — Saturday, October 31, 2026

Covers items published roughly from Friday morning, October 30, to Saturday morning, October 31, 2026 (Kyiv time). Items already covered in earlier briefs are left out.

## Top items

- **[Defense tech] Brave1 and two ministries open grant competitions for air-to-air missiles, Ukrainian radars and turbojet engines.** The Defence Ministry, the Digital Ministry and the Brave1 defence-tech cluster are offering up to UAH 75M per maker for guided air-to-air missiles, including versions launched from drones. They are offering up to UAH 100M per maker for Ukrainian radars that can spot jet-powered drones early. A third track covers cheaper turbojet engines, with no amount published. Applications close November 5, and for the first time they go through a Prozorro module. ([Defender Media](https://thedefender.media/en/2026/10/brave1-radars-jet-engines-missiles/), [Militarnyi](https://militarnyi.com/en/news/air-to-air-missiles-radar-ukraine-new-grants-brave1/))
- **[Crowdfunding] The Prytula Foundation hands over UAH 69.8M worth of reconnaissance and strike drones paid for by donors.** The batch went to seven units. It includes 45 Black Betty loitering munitions, a cheaper version of the RAM-2X from Ukrainian maker Ukrspecsystems, plus Shark-M reconnaissance systems and RAM-2X systems. Donations to the foundation's "Jaws" campaign paid for it. ([Defender Media](https://thedefender.media/en/2026/10/black-betty/))
- **[Drones] KT-2 FPV drones from a Ukrainian-Japanese company are now on Brave1 Market.** The maker is Kurin Technologies, a little-known Ukrainian developer. Japan's Terra Drone bought half of it in September for a reported $3.5M. Units can buy daytime and thermal versions with ePoints, the state's points-based ordering scheme. ([Defender Media](https://thedefender.media/en/2026/10/kt2-brave1-market/))
- **[OSINT] InformNapalm and other OSINT researchers warn that published photos and job ads can reveal where Ukraine's weapons plants are.** Euromaidan Press, building on reporting by the outlet Oboronka, says enemy analysts can piece together sites from details in photos and TV footage, job ads, staff profiles and file metadata. InformNapalm researcher Andrii Lisitsyn says blurring is not enough and recommends "cropping or fully opaque masking." Researchers also urge firms to strip metadata and stop using banned Russian software such as 1C. ([Euromaidan Press](https://euromaidanpress.com/2026/10/30/shadows-roofs-job-ads-give-ukrainian-arms-plants-away-to-russian-analysts-ukrainian-experts-warn/))
- **[Defense tech] The EU plans its first two drone technology hubs and points to Ukrainian know-how as a key advantage.** EU Economy Commissioner Valdis Dombrovskis said the pilot hubs, part of the DECODER project, will open in Latvia and the Netherlands in 2027. Ukraine is one of only two non-EU members of DECODER. The EU also aims to sign a Defence Industry Pact with Ukraine by the end of 2026 to make joint ventures and technology transfer easier. ([Kyiv Independent](https://kyivindependent.com/eu-announces-two-new-drone-technology-hubs-backed-by-ukrainian-know-how/))
- **[Defense tech] Former Tech Force of Ukraine head Kateryna Mykhalko presents a "Drone Security Index" for Europe.** Her organisation, New Age Defence, built the index with the policy group BRDO. It scores how ready European countries are for drone threats across prevention, detection, countermeasures and recovery. It uses open data and expert consultations, and looks at real capabilities rather than strategy papers. ([dev.ua](https://dev.ua/news/drone-security-index))

## Worth watching

Watch which companies win grants after the November 5 deadline.
