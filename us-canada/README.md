# US–Canada Pressure: Where We Are

*Last updated: October 20, 2026. The status and daily log below are updated each morning, Halifax time.*

**Canada is in a coercion phase.** That means an open trade war, annexation talk, and courting of separatists. No military moves have been seen. Using Russia's path to Crimea as a yardstick, we are at **stages 1–4 of 8**. Stages 5 and 6 are partly visible: there is open American commentary (researchers found no covert campaign as of September 10), and there are threats over NORAD. Nothing has been seen at the military stages, 7 and 8.

This tracker is written from a Canadian civic-tech point of view. It sticks to public reporting and labels each claim as an official statement, as reported, or unverified.

## Escalation signals we watch for

- **Pretext building:** US officials repeating claims of Canadian "interference," or saying US people or interests in Canada are under threat.
- **Recognition talk:** a senior US official (not staff or outside figures) offering recognition, money or credit to a separatist movement.
- **Contesting the vote:** separatists or US figures calling the Alberta result rigged after official results come out.
- **Covert operations:** researchers (MEO, DisinfoWatch) or CSE/SITE reporting coordinated inauthentic networks linked to the US.
- **Infrastructure leverage:** threats to energy, payments, cloud or telecom services that Canadians rely on.
- **Defence decoupling:** formal steps to change or leave NORAD, or to cut intelligence sharing.
- **Military moves:** unusual US deployments near the border or in the Arctic, or Canadian reserve call-ups.

## Civic tech projects

**Now (next 2–4 weeks)**

- A public log of what US officials say about Canada: who said what, when, with a link.
- A neutral "how Alberta's count works" explainer that links only to Elections Alberta sources.
- Mirrors of civic projects hosted outside US platforms, so they survive an account or platform cut-off.

**Short term (1–6 months)**

- An archive of political ads aimed at Canadians, flagging advertisers based in the US. Partner: McGill's Media Ecosystem Observatory.
- Short, sourced explainers that answer recurring narratives before they spread. Partners: DisinfoWatch and local newsrooms.
- A directory of Canadian-hosted tech alternatives, with migration guides for nonprofits and municipalities.
- A tracker of tariff actions and the sectors they hit, linked to official notices.
- Watch for tech or cyber roles in the military's planned civilian volunteer reserve, which was reported in draft in Nov 2025 ([CTV](https://www.ctvnews.ca/canada/article/the-military-plans-for-a-volunteer-force-to-defend-canada/)).

**Longer term (6+ months)**

- Community communications and alerting that keep working through outages, drawing on Ukraine's civilian experience.
- A Canadian-controlled tech stack for civil-society groups, built by contributing to existing open-source projects.
- A citable dataset of influence operations, with each item labelled verified or claimed.

## Ground rules

- Defensive and lawful only: no hacking back, DDoS attacks or leaks.
- Track public officials and organizations, never private individuals.
- Label every claim as verified, reported or self-reported, and leave attribution to established researchers.
- Stay nonpartisan on separation. The focus is foreign pressure and the integrity of the vote.
